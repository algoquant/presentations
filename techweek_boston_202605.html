Quant Hedge Fund Strategies

  • Trend Following Betting that prices will continue their trend up or down.

  • Mean Reversion Betting that prices will revert to their historical averages.

  • Statistical Arbitrage Betting that stock portfolios will revert to their historical averages.

  • Short Volatility Betting that prices will be range-bound and volatility will be contained.

  • Momentum Investing in the best performing stocks and shorting the worst performing ones.

  • Portfolio Momentum Investing in the best performing portfolios.

Estimating Volatility

  • Accurate and timely estimation of volatility is a key requirement of quant trading

  • Exponential Moving Average (EMA) Volatility

    \[\sigma^2_t = \lambda \sigma^2_{t-1} + (1 - \lambda) r^2_t\]

    \(r_t\) is the return at time \(t\)
    \(\lambda\) is the decay factor

  • Downside Volatility

    Only negative returns represent risk, not positive returns.

    \[\sigma^2_t = \lambda \sigma^2_{t-1} + (1 - \lambda) r^2_t\]

    \(r_t\) is the negative return at time \(t\)
    \(\lambda\) is the decay factor

  • Range Volatility Use the OHLC bars of prices

    \[\sigma^2_t = \lambda \sigma^2_{t-1} + (1 - \lambda) (H_t - L_t)^2\]

    \(H_t\) and \(L_t\) are the high and low prices of the day

Bias-variance Tradeoff

Finding the best bias-variance tradeoff is a fundamental objective in quant trading

Large decay factor \(\lambda\): slower EMA updates, less variance but more bias (larger latency).

Small decay factor \(\lambda\): faster EMA updates, less bias (smaller latency) but more variance.

Bollinger Mean-reverting Strategy

Switch between long and short positions, depending on the Z-score of past EMA returns

The z-score is equal to the ratio of the EMA return divided by the EMA volatility:

\[Z_t = \frac{r^{EMA}_t}{\sigma_t}\]

Bollinger strategy:

Go long if the z-score is below the lower threshold.

Go short if the z-score is above the upper threshold.

Bollinger Strategy Performance

The Bollinger strategy performs well in periods of high volatility, when prices are dropping

The Bollinger strategy is negatively correlated with the stock returns so it provides diversification and alpha.

Volatility Timing Strategy

Maintain a long position and adjust the size depending on the level of volatility

Increase the size when the volatility is low.

Decrease the size when the volatility is high.

Regime Switching Strategy

Switch between a long position and a mean-reverting strategy, depending on the level of volatility

Maintain a long position when the volatility is low.

Apply a mean-reversion strategy when the volatility is high.
